# Zawanah's Personal Website — Planning Brief

## 1. Purpose of this document

This document captures the decisions, rationale, working copy, content structure, and unresolved questions agreed during discovery. It is the reference brief for the website build and should be updated as decisions change.

No website implementation has begun.

## 2. Website purpose

The website will be Zawanah's professional home and personal publication. It should demonstrate how she thinks, what she builds, how she learns, and who she is beyond work.

The site should help:

- Senior technology leaders at multinational companies understand Zawanah's value and consider her for relevant roles.
- Prospective clients discover her work and approach her about speaking, AI workflow design, or consulting.
- Other builders relate to her experiments and share their own experiences.
- Readers follow her writing across AI, leadership, notes, and personal interests.

The site should not present itself as a conventional portfolio, online résumé, or generic AI consultancy. It should feel like a thoughtful personal publication with a builder's workshop attached.

## 3. Professional positioning

### Public name

**Zawanah**

The first name is distinctive enough to be the primary public identity.

### Professional title

**AI Solutions Designer**

This title was previously workshopped by Zawanah and best represents the intended direction. It accommodates:

- Designing AI-enabled workflows
- Building working applications and agent systems
- Applying Claude, ChatGPT, Claude Code, and Codex to real workflows
- Translating business and stakeholder needs into practical solutions
- Collaborating with technical and nontechnical people
- Consulting and speaking
- Developing solution architecture skills through the Claude Certified Architect certification

The site should not use **AI Engineer** as the primary title because Zawanah is not positioning herself as a traditional software engineer. It should not use **AI Enthusiast**, because that understates her existing builds and practical experience.

### Career context

Zawanah began her career in audit, earned a Chartered Accountant qualification, and then pivoted into data analytics through a bootcamp where she ranked among the top participants in her cohort.

At Aon, she worked as a Business Analytics Partner across five markets. Her responsibilities included:

- Analysing country and market data
- Presenting data-driven insights to senior executives
- Recommending service improvements
- Considering team restructuring to improve client delivery
- Identifying revenue opportunities using market data
- Managing stakeholders across multiple markets

She later began working extensively with Claude and ChatGPT for personal productivity and became deeply interested in emerging AI technologies and their practical possibilities.

The Chartered Accountant identity should **not** be foregrounded on the homepage or used as Zawanah's present-day professional label. Relevant strengths from that background—rigor, analysis, judgment, and quality—may be communicated without centring the qualification.

### Current practical experience

Zawanah has built:

- A NanoClaw personal assistant
- A NanoClaw executive coach
- A NanoClaw diary
- A NanoClaw home manager
- A Hermes personal agent
- A travel agent based on the Hermes setup

She was invited to speak about her NanoClaw agents. The talk received an average satisfaction score of **9.5/10** and exclusively positive feedback. She enjoyed sharing her work and wants the website to create more opportunities for discussion, recognition, speaking, and exchange with other builders.

### Differentiating strengths

The site should establish that Zawanah:

- Learns quickly and can pivot successfully into unfamiliar domains.
- Tries to understand new technologies deeply rather than following surface-level trends.
- Builds and tests real systems instead of only commenting on them.
- Combines hands-on AI experimentation with stakeholder and project-management skills.
- Can translate messy problems into clear recommendations for senior stakeholders.
- Cares about quality, finesse, and the practical usefulness of her work.
- Values smart, kind, and genuinely collaborative people.
- Offers a non-conventional, authentic perspective that is grounded in lived experience rather than generic AI-generated commentary.

## 5. Core content architecture

The four publishing categories are locked:

### Ideas

Leadership, technology, work, decision-making, and considered perspectives.

“Ideas” was selected over “Thoughts” because it feels more intentional, useful, and developed while still allowing personal and exploratory writing.

### Builds

Agents, experiments, workflows, technical demonstrations, analytical evaluations, and lessons from making things.

Builds may contain named recurring series rather than being divided into additional top-level categories.

Potential series include:

- **The Skills Series** — what a skill does, when to use it, and demonstrated outputs
- **Slash Commands** — focused workflow improvements
- **NanoClaw Diaries** — NanoClaw agents and lessons learned
- **Building with Hermes** — the evolving Hermes setup
- **The Analyst in the Loop** — evaluating AI-assisted analysis against Zawanah's own analysis and judgment

Series names remain provisional until the relevant content is developed.

### Notes

Certifications, talks, unfamiliar domains, lessons in progress, and reflections on learning quickly. The Claude Certified Architect journey is expected to be an early feature.

### After Hours

Personal interests and projects, including town-to-city builds. This provides a more human view of Zawanah without weakening the site's professional positioning.

### About

About is a navigation destination but **not** a publishing category. It will explain Zawanah's story, current professional proposition, working approach, and ways to contact her.

### Working navigation

`Zawanah / Ideas / Builds / Notes / After Hours / About`

The final placement of the portrait and theme control will be decided during design.

## 6. Content strategy

### Main publishing principle

The site should demonstrate expertise through specific work, thoughtful evaluation, and honest lessons. It should avoid unsupported claims and generic commentary about AI.

### Analytical workflows

Zawanah plans to publish analyst-related AI workflows, including comparisons such as:

- Using Opus versus Sonnet to analyse the same dataset
- Comparing model outputs with Zawanah's personal analysis
- Evaluating which model performs better for different analytical tasks
- Identifying where expert judgment remains necessary

These belong under **Builds**, potentially within **The Analyst in the Loop** series.

Potential article titles:

- *The Analyst in the Loop: Opus and Sonnet Take On the Same Dataset*
- *Same Data, Different Model, Very Different Confidence*
- *Opus Found the Pattern. Sonnet Explained It Better.*
- *I Gave Two Models the Same Dataset. Then I Checked Their Work.*

These are examples only and should not be treated as published titles.

### Repeatable structure for build articles

`Problem → Why it mattered → Approach → What happened → What I learned → What I'd change`

Build articles may optionally include:

- The category and named series
- Reading time and publication date
- A short premise
- “What I tested”
- “What changed my mind”
- Build details and selected outputs
- Related articles
- A restrained author block with Zawanah's portrait

### Backlinking

Articles should link naturally to relevant previous and related articles. There should not be a forced recurring sign-off; each article should end on its strongest thought.

## 8. Creative direction

### Selected direction

**Modern Editorial**, enriched with notebook-style experimental annotations and disciplined case-study structure.

The site should feel like an independent publication authored by an AI Solutions Designer. It should not resemble a generic developer portfolio, résumé template, dashboard, or AI consultancy landing page.

### Intended impression

- Thoughtful
- Credible
- Original
- Elegant
- Technically curious
- Enterprise-relevant
- Warm without being casual
- Worth returning to for new writing

### Visual language

- Editorial serif typography for major headlines
- Clean sans-serif typography for body copy, navigation, and technical details
- Generous whitespace
- Thin rules and clear editorial hierarchy
- Limited use of card containers
- Warm off-white rather than stark white in light mode
- Deep charcoal rather than pure black in dark mode
- Mint-green accent color
- Restrained, purposeful motion
- Small notebook-like annotations for experiments and works in progress
- Disciplined case-study presentation for builds

### Mint accent

Mint green is a locked requirement. It should be used selectively for:

- Links
- Category labels
- Active navigation states
- Focus indicators
- Small rules or annotations
- Selected interactive details

Mint should not become a large decorative wash. The light theme will require a darker, more saturated mint for accessible text contrast; the dark theme may use a brighter mint. Exact color values remain to be selected and tested.

### Theme support

The site must provide light and dark modes with a visible theme switch.

Requirements:

- Both modes must feel intentionally designed.
- Content readability and accessible contrast must be maintained in both modes.
- Dark mode should not be a simple color inversion.
- Theme preference should follow the visitor's system preference initially and remember an explicit choice where appropriate.
- Interactive and focus states must remain clearly visible in both modes.

### Visual patterns to avoid

- Excessive gradients
- Glowing cards
- Stereotypical AI imagery
- Badge walls
- Overly rounded floating-card interfaces
- Decorative technical diagrams with no informational purpose
- Dense dashboard-like layouts
- Heavy emoji use
- Visual treatments commonly associated with generic “vibe-coded” websites

## 10. Homepage plan

The homepage should not present four equal category boxes. It should establish Zawanah's proposition first and then curate the strongest work.

### Proposed hierarchy

1. **Compact identity header**  
   Zawanah's name and portrait, primary navigation, About, and theme control.

2. **Opening proposition**  
   A concise explanation of who Zawanah is, what she designs and builds, and why her perspective is distinctive.

3. **Lead feature**  
   One substantial build or article presented as the primary editorial story.

4. **Selected work**  
   A curated mixture of Ideas, Builds, and Notes. Entries should include category, title, short premise, and date where appropriate.

5. **Current experiments**  
   Compact notebook-like entries showing what Zawanah is testing, building, or learning now.

6. **After Hours feature**  
   One visually distinct but restrained personal entry.

7. **Closing invitation**  
   A subtle opening for speaking, consulting, workflow design, or collaboration.

### Credibility sequence

`Who Zawanah is → How she thinks → Evidence that she builds → Evidence that she keeps learning → The person beyond the work`

### Initial editorial lineup for the mock

The first mock should use the following real subject matter rather than generic portfolio placeholders.

#### Lead feature — NanoClaw

NanoClaw should be the homepage lead because it provides the strongest evidence of technical curiosity, architectural understanding, and the ability to explain a system clearly. “Strongest” in this context means the story that best demonstrates Zawanah's depth and judgment, not necessarily the build with the most features.

The story should focus on Zawanah going beneath the visible “typing...” experience to understand what the agent was doing underneath. It can draw on the personal assistant, executive coach, diary, and home manager as examples of how the same architecture serves different use cases.

Working headline directions:

- *What Happens Underneath “Typing...”*
- *I Built Four Personal Agents. Then I Went Looking Under the Hood.*
- *NanoClaw, Beyond the Chat Window*

The mock should use one strong title and a concise premise; the final title will be selected during copy development.

Available supporting material includes:

- A detailed NanoClaw standard and recommended setup guide with terminal screenshots
- A demonstration of the NanoClaw personal assistant creating a calendar event from a natural-language message
- A personal-assistant example using the request: “Art Science Museum with Michelle on 6 June, the entire afternoon”
- Documented assistant capabilities including creating, cancelling, or postponing events; detecting conflicts; summarising the coming week; sending a scheduled weekly summary; and reading dates from PDFs or images for calendar use

The personal assistant should be the concrete use case anchoring the broader architecture story. The editorial angle should connect a visibly simple interaction—a sentence sent through chat—to Zawanah's investigation of the system operating underneath “typing...”.

The lead feature should not become a setup tutorial on the homepage. The setup guide and technical screenshots belong within the full article, while the homepage should foreground the problem, the interaction, and the architectural curiosity.

#### Current experiment — Travel agent

The travel agent should appear as a current, use-case-led experiment. It was built to support a real trip planned for August, illustrating that Zawanah starts with a need and selects or builds the appropriate system rather than building technology for its own sake.

Working label: **In progress / Preparing for August**

#### Ideas feature — Radical empathy and Captain Pike

An Ideas article will explore the leadership quality of radical empathy through Captain Pike in *Star Trek: Strange New Worlds*. The article should use the episode as an entry point into Zawanah's own view of leadership rather than becoming a television recap.

Working headline directions:

- *What Captain Pike Understands About Radical Empathy*
- *Radical Empathy Is Not Soft Leadership*
- *The Kind of Leader I Want to Become Is Commanding the Enterprise*

The inspiration is *Star Trek: Strange New Worlds*, Season 1, Episode 5. Zawanah's central interpretation is that radical empathy is a **proactive approach to understanding others**.

The article should develop that idea in Zawanah's own voice: empathy is not only reacting kindly after someone explains themselves; it can mean actively doing the work to understand another person's context before making a judgment or decision. Specific plot details and any quotations must be verified before publication.

#### Analytical build — Opus versus Sonnet

The Sonnet analysis has already been completed using mock data from a previous technical assessment. Zawanah intends to repeat the analysis with Opus and compare both model outputs with her own analysis.

This should become an early **The Analyst in the Loop** article and should evaluate more than which model produces the most polished answer. The comparison can consider accuracy, analytical choices, assumptions, clarity, confidence, missed context, and the amount of expert correction required.

The existing Sonnet analysis is titled *My Analysis vs Claude Sonnet 5 in Excel on Service Delivery Optimization*. Zawanah used one-shot prompting and gave Claude the same dataset and objective she had previously addressed independently.

The mock dataset contains:

- Service-centre visits, including arrival time, time served, queue number, topics queried, and contact number
- Call-centre records, including questions asked, officer name, age range, gender, and contact number
- Customer-satisfaction scores for each case
- Demographic data linked by contact number

The assessment objective was to derive one or two insights and recommend a policy or process intervention that would improve customer experience. Zawanah's original response produced a two-pronged policy recommendation. Her reasons for testing Sonnet were to compare its independent approach with hers, identify aspects she may not have covered, and look for other innovative solutions.

The later Opus comparison should build on this completed Sonnet analysis instead of making the existing work appear unfinished.

Working headline directions:

- *Sonnet Analysed My Dataset. Now Opus Gets the Same Test.*
- *Same Dataset, Two Models, One Analyst Checking the Work*
- *Which Model Would I Trust With the Analysis?*

#### Notes feature — CCA-F applied

Learning content should extract useful concepts from the Claude Certified Architect — Foundations material and apply them to systems Zawanah has built or is designing. This prevents the certification journey from becoming a study log and shows the transfer from concept to practice.

The first example will examine hub-and-spoke architecture for a coordinator and its agents.

Working headline directions:

- *Hub, Spokes, and the Agent in Charge*
- *What Hub-and-Spoke Architecture Looks Like in an Agent System*
- *Learning the Pattern, Then Finding It in My Own Build*

#### After Hours feature — Town to City

After Hours will initially feature Zawanah's work in the game *Town to City*, where she exercises creativity by building and decorating a town as its mayor.

The first story can connect creative play with systems thinking: road planning matters, households need access to food, clothing, and leisure, and well-designed hubs make the town function more effectively.

Working headline directions:

- *I Became a Mayor. The Roads Immediately Became My Problem.*
- *A Pretty Town Still Needs a Functional Road Network*
- *What Building a Tiny Town Taught Me About Hubs*

This feature should remain playful and visual while avoiding a forced attempt to turn every hobby into a professional productivity lesson.

### Hosting and analytics decisions

- The initial site will be built for GitHub Pages and published at `https://zawanahs.github.io/`.
- The site will later use the custom domain `https://zawanah.com/`.
- Routing, canonical URLs, social metadata, feeds, and analytics should be designed so the domain transition does not require restructuring the content.
- Visitor analytics are required.
- Analytics should measure page and article views, category and series popularity, referral sources, and broad engagement trends.
- Analytics should remain lightweight and privacy-conscious. The provider will be selected during technical planning; the site should avoid invasive tracking or unnecessary personal data collection.

### Markdown-first publishing decision

The site will use Astro content collections and a Markdown-first publishing workflow.

Zawanah's normal publishing process should be:

1. Create or duplicate a `.md` article file in the appropriate content folder.
2. Complete a small metadata block at the top of the file.
3. Write the article in Markdown and add referenced images to the documented asset folder.
4. Commit and push the changes to GitHub.
5. GitHub Actions validates and builds the site, then publishes it automatically to GitHub Pages.

The content system should automatically derive the article page, category archive, series page, related-content links, sitemap, RSS entry, social metadata, and homepage eligibility from the article metadata. Zawanah should not need to edit page layouts or navigation for ordinary publishing.

Proposed article metadata:

```yaml
---
title: "What Happens Underneath Typing..."
description: "I built four personal agents, then went looking beneath the chat window."
published: 2026-08-10
updated:
category: builds
series: nanoclaw-diaries
tags:
  - agents
  - architecture
featured: true
draft: false
cover: ./images/nanoclaw-cover.jpg
coverAlt: "Description of the meaningful cover image"
---
```

Implementation requirements:

- Validate required metadata before deployment and fail with an understandable error when it is incomplete.
- Allow `draft: true` articles to remain unpublished.
- Generate stable human-readable URLs.
- Support Markdown for normal articles and optional MDX only when a specific article needs an interactive component.
- Provide a reusable article template and a short publishing guide.
- Preserve article URLs when the site moves from `zawanahs.github.io` to `zawanah.com`.
