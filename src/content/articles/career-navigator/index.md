I have made non-conventional career moves driven by curiosity and desire to explore emerging tools or problems that are interesting to me.

While the moves may seem clear to me, it's *not* clear to most people. 

Moreover, circumstances and opportunities that come may have aligned with my goals in the past but may not necessarily be so for my current goals.

> Does it function like a chatbot with résumé? No.
> 
> A résumé is a compressed summary written for an external audience. It **rarely** captures the full context behind my career: the projects I am proud of, the stakeholder feedback I have received, the environments in which I thrive, or the values that shape my decisions.

Having a Career Navigator who understands my career history and my current goals helps to provide perspectives when navigating career decisions over time.

## Professional Background, Goals, and Personality

I share with my career navigator the following information :

**1. Detailed career history and professional background**

This includes every project that I was involved in, and anything that demonstrates my technical skills and impact to stakeholder(s).

I also detail soft skills I have demonstrated in these projects through stories and, where possible, apply the STAR method (which would be useful for interviews).

> Note: While context is key, **keep in mind not to share private and confidential company information, personal identifiers, and anything you would not be comfortable storing in the system you use**.

**2. Goals and Values**

I share what are my current career goals and how I plan to achieve it. The plan or goal can be *refined* over time as I continue conversing and relaying my experiences with the Career Navigator. 

Perhaps my goals are not realistic in the timeframe that I plan to achieve, or perhaps my plan is not optimal given the current conditions... The Career Navigator will share its point of view and I will decide how to recalibrate my goals or plans.

**3. Personality assessment results**

Similar to the information I shared with my journalling agent, I included personality assessments that provide insights into how I work. This way, I understand myself better in the context of professional work, and I have a feedback loop for my blind spots. 

For example, I can be perfectionist and I will keep iterating over a piece of work forever, until I achieve what I envisioned but this is not optimal nor is it strategic with limited time or resource. By understanding my inclinations, it forces me to challenge what I *think* is the best way by providing alternate perspectives grounded by logic.

---

The Career Navigator is not *merely* storing these information, but it would be able to **reason across all the information that it knows about me through these artifacts**. 

This shared context improves the quality of the conversation I have with the Career Navigator, so it would not give generic advice that you get by simply chatting with any other LLMs.

> For example, it can critically answer:
> - Does this opportunity fit my values and preferred working environment?
> - Which of my experiences are most relevant for this role?
> - How should I position a non-linear career story?

That said, the Career Navigator should not be making my career decisions on my behalf. That is not the objective here. 

Its role is to be a **thinking partner when navigating my career**. It surfaces patterns, retrieve evidence, challenge inconsistencies and translate a large collection of experiences into a clear story. 

## Keep a running record of Impact

One of the most useful applications is **maintaining a current brag sheet**.

Achievements are easiest to document while details are still *fresh*. After several months, it would be difficult to remember the exact metric or the stakeholder's feedback. 

These entries become evidence that can :
- support a promotion case, 
- strengthen performance review, 
- improve a resume or 
- transformed into STAR stories for interviews.

## Bridge for Career Continuity

Careers are increasingly non-linear as people move between functions, industries and different forms of work. Most of us (or at least most that I know) develop valuable skills in side projects, volunteer roles, and experiments that may never appear in an official job description.

The Career Navigator provides *continuity* across all of these experiences. It helps identify the thread connecting them: the problems I choose to solve, the strengths I rely on, the environments where I perform best, and the impact that I want my work to have.

<mark>And I think this thread is what makes each of us unique in what we bring to the table. :)</mark>
